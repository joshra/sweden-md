# How Sweden Forms a Government After an Election: Minority Cabinets, Support Agreements, and the 4 Percent Threshold

> **30-second overview:** The Riksdag has 349 seats, so 175 is the majority line. But a Swedish government does not need to control 175 seats by itself. Minority cabinets, support agreements, and parliamentary tolerance are normal, which means parties near the 4 percent threshold can change the entire formation equation.

## Why it matters

If you read Swedish elections with a presidential-system instinct, it is easy to ask the wrong question: who won? The better question is: who can form a government that is not rejected by a parliamentary majority and can pass a budget?

This is worth understanding because Sweden turns election results through several institutional steps: votes become seats, seats become bargaining power, and bargaining becomes a prime minister and budget.

## The 4 percent threshold

Swedish proportional representation includes a national 4 percent threshold. The purpose is to balance representation with governability: enough parties can compete, but parliament does not fragment without limit.

The threshold makes parties near 4 percent very important. If a smaller party crosses it, it can bring a meaningful number of seats to its bloc and potentially decide who can govern. If it falls below, the same bloc may lose decisive seats. That is why threshold parties receive intense attention in Swedish campaigns.

## The 175-seat line and negative parliamentarism

The Riksdag has 349 seats, so 175 is an absolute majority. But Sweden does not require a prime-minister candidate to first receive 175 yes votes. The test is whether 175 members vote against the candidate. This is the logic usually described as negative parliamentarism.

That makes minority governments normal. A cabinet can govern without holding a majority, but it must prevent an absolute majority from rejecting it and must find enough support for budgets and major legislation.

## How minority governments work

A minority government is not automatically weak. It can secure predictable support through formal agreements, budget cooperation, or issue-by-issue exchange. The key is that supporting parties do not always need to enter cabinet. They can stay outside government while influencing policy documents and parliamentary votes.

The post-2022 Tido Agreement is the clearest recent example. The Moderates, Christian Democrats, and Liberals formed the government, while the Sweden Democrats supported it from outside cabinet. This created distance between who holds office and who influences policy.

## How to read 2026 after election night

After the 2026 election, readers should watch three things. First, which parties cross the 4 percent threshold. Second, whether the right or centre-left side approaches 175 seats. Third, whether the parties inside a possible governing basis are actually willing to sign or tolerate one arrangement.

That is the distinctive texture of Swedish post-election politics. Voters choose the materials; parties assemble the structure. The quantity of seats matters, but the assembly method matters just as much.
