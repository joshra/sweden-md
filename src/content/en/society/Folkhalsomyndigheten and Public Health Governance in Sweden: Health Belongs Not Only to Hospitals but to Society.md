# Folkhalsomyndigheten and Public Health Governance in Sweden: Health Belongs Not Only to Hospitals but to Society

> **30-second overview:** Outside Sweden, Folkhalsomyndigheten is often remembered mainly through pandemic press conferences. Inside the Swedish system, however, its role is much broader. It helps protect the country against infectious disease and health threats while also turning mental health, vaccination, addiction prevention, physical activity, and health inequality into fields that can be monitored, coordinated, and governed.

## Why It Matters

When I speak with Taiwanese readers about Sweden, I often notice that "public health" is still imagined primarily through hospitals, treatment, or emergency epidemic response. In Sweden, the idea is wider and more upstream. Public health is not only about what happens after people get sick. It is also about tracking population health, identifying risks early, and adjusting policy tools so that differences in birth conditions, education, work, housing, and daily habits do not quietly harden into deeper health inequality.

Folkhalsomyndigheten matters because it institutionalises that less visible but highly consequential work. For Taiwanese readers, understanding this agency is not simply a matter of learning one more Swedish authority. It is a way of seeing how Sweden expands "health" from a medical-sector concern into a state capacity shared across society.

## It Does Not Run Hospitals. It Turns Health into Cross-Sector Governance

According to the Government Offices and the agency's own mission statement, Folkhalsomyndigheten has national responsibility for public-health issues. Its task is to promote good and equitable health, prevent disease and injury, sustain effective communicable-disease control, and protect the population against different kinds of health threats. Its mandate is also explicitly tied to Sweden's public-health objective: creating social conditions for good and equitable health across the whole population and closing avoidable health gaps within a generation.

That institutional design is worth pausing on. Folkhalsomyndigheten is not a hospital-management agency, nor is it merely a producer of health-information campaigns. It functions more like a central node that translates health into governable state language: which risks deserve sustained attention, which indicators must be tracked, and where municipalities, regions, and national actors need to adjust together. In the Swedish view, health is part of social structure, not something the state addresses only after illness appears.

## Infectious-Disease Defence, Laboratory Capacity, and International Links Are Its Hardest Powers

At the same time, Folkhalsomyndigheten is not an abstract promoter of wellbeing. Its official remit makes clear that it carries overall national responsibility for communicable-disease protection, coordinates that work at the national level, and produces regulations, recommendations, and guidance for healthcare professionals and relevant systems. Its responsibilities include vaccination, preparedness for outbreaks, parts of the national reserve of anti-infective medicines, and efforts to reduce antimicrobial resistance and healthcare-associated infection.

What makes this especially important is that the agency also performs microbiological laboratory analyses and supports quality and method development for laboratories relevant to national disease control. In other words, it is not coordinating from a distance alone. It has its own scientific and technical capacity for surveillance and interpretation. From a Swedish perspective, that is logical. A state that wants coherent responses across ordinary times, crises, and cross-border disease threats cannot rely only on fragmented local reporting. It also needs central monitoring, laboratory strength, and international reporting channels.

## Swedish Public Health Does Not Watch Only Viruses. It Watches How Inequality Becomes Ill Health

Another defining feature is the breadth of the agency's agenda. Official mission texts and annual reporting show long-term work on mental health and suicide prevention, alcohol, narcotics, doping, tobacco, nicotine products, and gambling, as well as physical activity, healthy eating, sexual and reproductive health, antimicrobial resistance, and the monitoring of population health and its determinants.

This reflects a very characteristic Nordic governance logic. Sweden does not treat health as a matter of personal discipline alone. It keeps asking which social conditions make some groups more likely to become ill, lose health earlier, or struggle to access support. That is why Folkhalsomyndigheten does more than issue warnings. It builds the knowledge base that helps national, regional, and local actors place health policy inside schools, communities, planning, leisure systems, and wider social support structures.

## Its Core Tools Are Reports, Guidance, Monitoring Systems, and Coordination Rather Than a Single Command

If Socialstyrelsen resembles the standards-and-knowledge infrastructure of the welfare state, Folkhalsomyndigheten is closer to a radar and coordination platform for health risk. Through annual reports, thematic publications, monitoring systems, government assignments, and knowledge support to subnational actors, it keeps public health from becoming visible only during crisis. Its 2025 annual report, for example, emphasises expanded concrete support for knowledge-based public-health work at local, regional, and national levels, together with stronger structures for infectious-disease surveillance and laboratory preparedness.

That may sound less dramatic than emergency command, but it is a crucial part of the Swedish system. Health problems are rarely solved by a single order. They require accumulated data, repeated naming of problems, and coordination across layers of government. The value of Folkhalsomyndigheten therefore lies not in deciding everything for everyone else, but in helping the system know where to look and where to correct course early.

## A Taiwan-Facing Takeaway: Mature Public Health Governs Society Before the Crisis Arrives

What feels most instructive to me is that Folkhalsomyndigheten shows mature public-health governance is not only about commanding through emergency. It is about treating health as a public issue that crosses sectors, scales, and time horizons long before a crisis arrives. From vaccination to mental health, from surveillance to physical activity, from national preparedness to local implementation, Sweden tries to place seemingly separate issues inside one shared governance frame.

So the story of Folkhalsomyndigheten is not simply that Sweden has a public-health agency. It is that Sweden tries to make health something that belongs not only to hospitals and not only to private behaviour, but to a public order that society has to maintain together. That institutional sensibility is often closer to the real Sweden than any single policy slogan.
